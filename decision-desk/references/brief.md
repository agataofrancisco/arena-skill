# The brief (Step 1)

The council is only as good as the brief. It is one page, written before any lens speaks, and it must be **neutral**: facts and options, no analysis and no recommendation. If the brief argues for an answer, every lens inherits the bias and the council becomes a rubber stamp.

Write it to `desk-runs/<date>-<slug>/brief.md`.

## Template

```markdown
# Brief: [decision in a few words]

## The decision
[One line. A question with a verb and a chooser, not a topic: not "pricing" but
"raise the price from 2000 to 3500 for all clients, for new clients only, or not at all".]

## Options
- **A — [name]**: [what exactly happens, and to whom]
- **B — [name]**: [what exactly happens, and to whom]
- **C — keep doing what you do now**: [what that means in numbers]

[If the founder brought a single idea, add the smaller version of it as an option.
Always include the do-nothing option: it is a real option with a real cost.]

## The numbers
| what | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly revenue | | |
| Cash / runway (months) | | |
| Margin | | |
| Team size, and who is overloaded | | |
| Price, volume, conversion | | |
| Founder hours per week on this | | |

Mark anything you inferred as `(assumed)`. Numbers the council has to invent are numbers it will fight about.

## Constraints
[Deadline, budget, hours, contracts, people who cannot be moved, legal or regulatory limits,
anything that takes an option off the table even if it looks attractive.]

## What success looks like
[A number with a date. "45k a month by March", not "grow the business".]

## The lean
[Which way the founder is leaning, and why. The council tests this, it does not echo it.]

## Reversibility
[What is irreversible about the recommended option, and what it costs to undo. If the answer is
"nothing, we can stop next month", say so: it changes the standard of proof.]

## What has already been tried
[Attempts, results, and what they ruled out. Empty if nothing yet.]

## Not on the table
[Options the founder has already ruled out, and why, so no lens wastes a round proposing them.]

## Assumptions
[Every `(assumed)` from above, listed in one place so the founder can correct them in one pass.]
```

## Rules

- Under 300 words of prose. Tables do not count.
- One pass of questions, at most four, in a single batch. Then proceed with stated assumptions.
- Never put a recommendation, a leaning of your own, or a "probably" in the brief.
- If the founder cannot supply a number, write the range and say what would settle it. Do not invent precision.
- Re-read the brief before the verdict: every condition in the report must trace to something in it.
